Rook Industries · Dispatch · Final report

What happened with release 4.2

A change people asked for. A number that went down. Four responders who quietly stopped hearing from us. Six weeks of working out why, what we'd build instead, and how to stop it repeating.

Rook Industries is a fictional course scenario (Product School, cohort ccpm-2026.1). Data covers 29 Jun – 6 Sep 2026 only; all figures are from the course files.

Why it mattered

"Seasonal" couldn't be tested

With no baseline, Priya's seasonality theory was a hypothesis, not a finding. Naming the gaps up front kept me from asserting numbers I hadn't pulled.

Module 4 · X-Ray Vision

Finding: "recent acceptance" isn't a rate. It's a meter that only rises when you accept.

Prompt I wrote
Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on.

Result (history.py, offer.py): starts at 0.5. +0.08 per taken ping, −0.12 per turned-down or missed ping, clamped 0–1. Only record_accepted adds points. Nothing decays. An unsigned 2019 TODO about it was never decided.

What it means
~60%break-even
Accept less than ~60% of pings and the meter only goes down.
Illustrative drift with repeated misses. No recovery path unless pinged again.

4.2 weights: proximity 0.45 → 0.60, recent acceptance 0.40 → 0.25, capability 0.15 unchanged. Ping wait 90 → 60s.

Plainly: what happened

A responder who goes quiet has no way back.

Leading hypothesis, not yet confirmed with Wen Li or September data.

Ping wait cut to 60s
→
Slower answerers miss pings
→
Miss counts as a turn-down: −0.12
→
Rank falls, fewer pings
→
No pings = no way to earn points. Stuck.

What the data supports

Responders who kept 7+ pings a week after the dip recovered; the four who fell below ~4 never did. They were ordinary before 4.2 (69–82% acceptance), so not prior decliners.

Where the story is weaker

With the meter weighted less, a miss costs less rank than before. Proximity (are the four peripheral?) is a live alternative; I haven't run that query. "Slower responders" is an inference: I have no response times.

Still to ask

Wen Li: decay speed, whether the 12 Aug deploy reset in-memory scores. Marcus: his unanswered 14 Aug question. Ravi: where the four sit, and the midnight–6am drop.

Prompt I wrote
This prototype is very weak.... colors are weak... can show more of Rook's branding (even though not designed) We can show more of the "Handler Console" that we are logged in as... [...] For the responder screen, make it look more like an iphone...

Produced the two-screen prototype below: handler and responder kept apart so nobody confuses whose screen it is.

1 · Not-real misses

A handler can mark misses as not real, which restores rank.

2 · Ease back in

Reset to neutral, offer a few pings at a time. Any answer, even a turn-down, counts. Silent repeats pause and ask "still away?"

3 · Plain status

Handlers and responders both see in words why pings are or aren't coming.

If the frame is blank, open the prototype directly. All numbers, dates and tags in it are illustrative.

Does not

Revert 4.2 · change proximity weights · change ping wait · ping anyone marked away · identify anyone behind a cover.

Placeholders, not decisions

N=3 probe pings, K=3 silent offers, and the four ramp steps are mine. Helen hasn't decided whether "those misses weren't real" is in v1. Risk: someone marked available but unreachable costs a full ping wait.

Why it maps to 4.2

4.2 shipped two changes together with no stated way to see one responder's pings go to zero. A success-measure and owner check pushes that question to the surface before release. Wen Li's absence and Priya's exit left the change with no owner when it landed.

What it can't catch

It reads briefs, not code. It wouldn't have spotted the meter or the 60s timeout on its own. It enforces the question, not the answer.
